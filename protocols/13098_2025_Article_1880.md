# Pyroptosis-related bioinformatics analysis of diabetic wound healing

## Source

- Paper: *Identifying pyroptosis-related genes as novel therapeutic targets in diabetic foot ulceration*
- Citation: Liu K, Lei L, Yang X-l, Zhang X-h. *Diabetology & Metabolic Syndrome*. 2025;17:306.
- DOI: 10.1186/s13098-025-01880-9
- Source document: `13098_2025_Article_1880.pdf`

## Objective

Identify pyroptosis-related genes associated with diabetic wound healing (DWH), characterize their biological and immune context, select diagnostic features, construct a multigene diagnostic model, and assess its performance in a second expression dataset.

This protocol extracts the paper's bioinformatics pipeline only. Tissue collection and qPCR validation are not computational stages and are therefore outside scope.

## Study design

### Expression datasets

- GSE147890, platform GPL571, skin tissue:
  - 12 DWH samples and 12 control samples.
  - The selected samples comprise 5 Control-0 h, 7 Control-24 h, 6 Diabetic-0 h, and 6 Diabetic-24 h samples.
  - Treat this dataset as the development dataset for discovery, feature selection, model fitting, immune analysis, and risk-group analysis.
- GSE80178, platform GPL16686, foot skin tissue:
  - 9 DWH samples and 3 control samples.
  - Treat this dataset as the independent dataset used by the paper for expression and diagnostic-performance validation.

Retrieve expression data, platform annotation, phenotype metadata, and accession metadata from GEO. Preserve accession-provided sample identifiers and document the exact mapping from samples to DWH/control groups.

### Pyroptosis-related gene set

Reconstruct the paper's pyroptosis-related gene (PRG) universe from the following sources:

1. Query GeneCards with `Pyroptosis`, retain protein-coding genes, and record the retrieval date and the evidence or relevance field used. The paper reports 575 genes from this source.
2. Identify pyroptosis-related genes from PubMed literature using `Pyroptosis` as the keyword and document the reproducible selection procedure.
3. Merge the two sources, normalize gene symbols, and remove duplicates. The paper reports 582 unique PRGs.

Prefer the exact 582-gene list in Supplementary Table S2 when available. Do not substitute a contemporary database result without labeling the version and resulting difference from the paper's list.

## Analysis pipeline

### 1. Acquire and annotate expression data

Download GSE147890 and GSE80178 using GEOquery 2.70.0 or a contract-equivalent method. Map platform probes to current gene symbols using the corresponding GPL annotation. Record how unmapped probes, probes mapping to multiple genes, and multiple probes mapping to one gene are handled.

Required outputs:

- Expression matrix for each accession before processing.
- Sample metadata and explicit group assignments.
- Probe-to-gene mapping table.
- Acquisition provenance, including source URLs, retrieval time, and checksums where practical.

### 2. Normalize data and assess batch effects

For each dataset, inspect expression distributions with box plots and assess structure with principal component analysis before correction. Normalize and standardize expression values using limma 3.58.1. Apply ComBat from sva 3.50.0 only when a defensible batch variable exists in the accession metadata; record the batch and biological design matrices.

Repeat box plots and PCA after processing. Keep the two accessions separate for differential expression rather than merging their matrices across platforms.

Quality gates:

- Group labels and any batch labels must not be confounded without an explicit warning.
- The transformation state and normalization method must be recorded.
- PCA and distribution plots must be produced before and after any correction.
- Biological group signal must not be removed solely to make dataset distributions similar.

### 3. Differential expression within each dataset

Fit a DWH-versus-control contrast independently in GSE147890 and GSE80178 using limma 3.58.1.

Use the paper's discovery rule:

- nominal two-sided P < 0.05;
- upregulated when logFC > 0;
- downregulated when logFC < 0.

Report Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted P values in addition to the paper's nominal-P selection. The paper's `|logFC| > 0` condition imposes no meaningful effect-size threshold and must be labeled as such.

Required outputs:

- Complete differential-expression table for each dataset.
- Selected DEG table for each dataset.
- Volcano plot for each dataset.
- Counts of upregulated and downregulated genes.

Paper-reported checkpoints:

- GSE147890: 1,336 DEGs, comprising 820 upregulated and 516 downregulated genes.
- GSE80178: 2,727 DEGs, comprising 1,277 upregulated and 1,450 downregulated genes.

### 4. Identify pyroptosis-related DEGs

Intersect the selected DEGs from both datasets with the PRG universe:

`PRDEGs = DEGs(GSE147890) intersection DEGs(GSE80178) intersection PRGs`

Produce an intersection diagram and heatmaps of the resulting genes in both datasets.

Paper-reported checkpoint: 9 PRDEGs — PINK1, FSTL1, ULK1, HDAC3, NOD2, CPTP, TLR3, RELA, and NLRX1.

### 5. GO and KEGG over-representation analysis

Perform GO Biological Process, Cellular Component, and Molecular Function analysis and KEGG pathway analysis on the PRDEGs using clusterProfiler 4.10.0 or a contract-equivalent implementation.

Use:

- nominal P < 0.05;
- FDR q value < 0.25;
- Homo sapiens identifiers;
- an explicitly reported tested-gene universe.

Produce complete result tables and compact pathway visualizations. The paper highlights IKK/NF-kappaB signaling, JNK signaling, pattern-recognition receptor signaling, mitophagy, influenza A, NOD-like receptor signaling, neurodegeneration-related pathways, and inflammatory bowel disease.

### 6. Whole-transcriptome GSEA for DWH versus control

Using GSE147890, rank all tested genes by DWH-versus-control logFC and perform preranked GSEA with clusterProfiler 4.10.0 against the MSigDB C2 canonical-pathway collection reported as `Cp.All.V2022.1` for Homo sapiens.

Use:

- random seed 2020;
- 1,000 permutations;
- minimum gene-set size 10;
- maximum gene-set size 500;
- Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted P < 0.05;
- FDR < 0.25.

Record the exact GMT filename and checksum because the collection label in the paper is not a complete canonical MSigDB filename. Produce the complete enrichment table and representative enrichment plots.

### 7. Screen diagnostic genes

Use GSE147890 and the PRDEGs to perform the following sequential feature-selection procedure:

1. Fit univariate logistic regressions for DWH status and retain genes with P < 0.05.
2. Fit a random forest with seed 234 and 300 trees.
3. Rank variables by Mean Decrease Gini.
4. Select the optimal RF feature count through repeated cross-validation.
5. Apply LASSO logistic regression to the RF-screened genes using glmnet 4.1-8, seed 300, and the paper-reported 200 iterations.
6. Retain nonzero LASSO features as key genes.

Prevent leakage: all outcome-informed screening, parameter tuning, and cutoff selection must be confined to GSE147890. Do not use GSE80178 to select features or refit coefficients.

Required outputs:

- Univariate logistic-regression results.
- RF error curve and importance table/plot.
- Cross-validation performance by feature count.
- LASSO coefficient path, cross-validation curve, selected penalty, and retained features.
- Random seeds and resampling indices.

Paper-reported checkpoint: FSTL1, PINK1, HDAC3, ULK1, CPTP, and NOD2 are the six selected genes.

### 8. Fit and evaluate the diagnostic model

Fit a multivariable logistic regression model in GSE147890 using all selected genes. Calculate each sample's linear predictor as:

`RiskScore = sum(coefficient_i * expression_i)`

Freeze the preprocessing transformations, gene list, coefficients, and score direction from GSE147890, then apply them unchanged to GSE80178.

Produce:

- Model coefficient table and formula.
- Nomogram using rms 6.7-1.
- Calibration analysis in the development data, with the resampling method recorded.
- Decision-curve analysis using ggDCA 1.1.
- ROC curves and AUC values using pROC 1.18.5 for GSE147890 and GSE80178.
- Confidence intervals for AUC and, where estimable, sensitivity and specificity at a development-set-defined threshold.

Paper-reported model:

`RiskScore = -29.9355*FSTL1 + 149.1502*PINK1 + 12.0898*ULK1 + 73.8888*HDAC3 + 30.7392*NOD2 + 39.5365*CPTP`

Paper-reported checkpoint: AUC 1.000 in both datasets. Treat this as a high-overfitting-risk result because both cohorts are small, especially the three-control validation cohort.

### 9. Verify and characterize key genes

In both datasets:

- Compare each key gene's expression between DWH and control groups.
- Plot gene-level ROC curves and calculate AUC values.
- Use GOSemSim 2.28.0 to calculate GO semantic similarity among key genes.
- Use RCircos 1.2.2 to visualize chromosomal locations.

Produce group-comparison statistics and plots, complete ROC tables, the semantic-similarity matrix, and chromosome-location data and figure.

### 10. Estimate immune-cell infiltration

Apply ssGSEA to GSE147890 to estimate the relative abundance of the paper's 28 immune-cell types. Preserve the exact immune signature gene sets and their provenance.

The agent is authorized to resolve the paper's unnamed signature source without
another user prompt. Select an authoritative published human 28-cell immune
metagene reference only when its method and taxonomy match this stage;
Charoentong et al. Table S6 is preferred when available. If the authoritative
source is supplied as a workbook rather than the node's runtime format, create
a deterministic audited adapter that extracts every metagene/cell-type pair to
the node's required two-column gene-set database. Preserve and checksum the
original workbook, record its citation and source sheet/range, verify exactly
28 nonempty cell-type sets and all membership counts, and label the derived
file as agent-generated remediation. This authority does not permit replacing
the requested 28-cell ssGSEA taxonomy with LM22 or another method.

Then:

- Compare immune-cell scores between DWH and control groups.
- Calculate Spearman correlations among immune-cell scores.
- Calculate Spearman correlations between key-gene expression and immune-cell scores.
- Use two-sided P < 0.05 for the paper-aligned significance display and also report multiple-testing-adjusted values.

Produce the score matrix, group-comparison table/plot, immune-cell correlation heatmap, and key-gene/immune-cell correlation table and bubble plot.

Paper-reported checkpoints include six significantly different immune-cell types, a positive FSTL1–central-memory-CD4-T-cell correlation (r = 0.78), and a negative ULK1–Th2-cell correlation (r = -0.82).

### 11. Perform high- versus low-risk GSEA

Within DWH samples from GSE147890, divide samples at the median development-set risk score into high- and low-risk groups. Perform high-versus-low differential analysis with limma and rank all tested genes by logFC for GSEA.

Use the same MSigDB collection, seed, permutation count, gene-set-size limits, and significance criteria as in Stage 6. Report the exact number of DWH samples in each risk group and the tie-handling rule at the median.

Produce the risk-group assignments, complete differential-expression table, ranked list, GSEA result table, and representative enrichment plots.

The paper highlights canonical retinoid cycle in rods, hematopoietic stem-cell differentiation, peptide ligand-binding receptors, and NABA secreted factors.

### 12. Predict regulatory interaction networks

For the six key genes:

- Query ENCORI 3.0 for interacting miRNAs and retain interactions with `pancancerNum > 12`.
- Query ChIPBase 3.0 for transcription factors binding the key genes and retain records for which the sum of upstream and downstream sample counts is greater than 6.
- Standardize identifiers and construct separate mRNA-miRNA and mRNA-TF networks.

Record retrieval dates, query parameters, database versions, raw query results, and network edge tables. Produce reproducible network visualizations rather than relying on manual Cytoscape editing.

Paper-reported checkpoints:

- mRNA-miRNA network: 3 key genes and 43 miRNAs.
- mRNA-TF network: 5 key genes and 32 transcription factors.

## Statistical conventions

- Use R 4.2.2 for paper-aligned reproduction unless an equivalent environment is explicitly documented.
- Use two-sided tests.
- Use independent-samples t tests for normally distributed two-group comparisons and Mann-Whitney U tests otherwise.
- Use Kruskal-Wallis tests for three or more groups where applicable.
- Use Spearman correlation for molecular associations.
- Use P < 0.05 unless a stage defines a different criterion.
- Apply Benjamini-Hochberg correction for multiple testing and retain both nominal and adjusted values.
- Report effect sizes and uncertainty, not only P values.

## Reproducibility requirements

- Record every downloaded resource, release/version, retrieval time, and checksum.
- Preserve raw data unchanged.
- Record sample inclusion, exclusion, group assignment, time-point handling, and probe aggregation decisions.
- Record random seeds, software/package versions, effective parameters, model objects, and resampling indices.
- Save complete machine-readable result tables underlying every figure.
- Distinguish paper-aligned results from sensitivity analyses or corrective alternatives.
- Every successful analysis stage must include its required reproducibility code bundle.

## Known ambiguities and limitations in the paper

1. The exact 582-gene PRG list depends on Supplementary Table S2; the GeneCards/PubMed curation procedure is not fully reproducible from the article alone.
2. The batch covariates supplied to ComBat are not identified. Do not infer a batch variable from outcome labels.
3. Probe-to-gene aggregation rules are not reported.
4. The differential-expression cutoff `|logFC| > 0` is effectively only nominal P < 0.05 and does not enforce biological effect size.
5. The RF resampling description conflicts: the Methods report five-fold, ten-repeat cross-validation, whereas the Results describe five tenfold cross-validations.
6. The LASSO family, precise meaning of 200 iterations, penalty-selection rule, and full preprocessing recipe are not specified.
7. The MSigDB collection label is incomplete or noncanonical; the exact GMT release must be recovered and pinned.
8. The ssGSEA 28-cell signature source and gene list are not named. Stage 10
   therefore delegates source resolution to the agent under its stated
   authoritative-reference and audited-conversion constraints; any remaining
   scientifically distinct candidates must be reported as a blocker.
9. It is unclear whether calibration and decision-curve analyses were internally validated or calculated on the same data used for model fitting.
10. GSE80178 is called an external validation cohort in the Methods, while the Discussion also states that an independent external validation cohort was lacking. Report this inconsistency.
11. The very small cohorts and perfect reported AUCs create substantial overfitting and uncertainty risk; the validation results should not be interpreted as clinical validation.
12. High/low risk groups are a post-model stratification of DWH samples, not a validated prognostic endpoint.

## Completion criteria

The pipeline is complete when all twelve computational stages have either produced their required artifacts or are explicitly marked blocked with the missing resource identified. Compare reproduced results with every paper-reported checkpoint, and report all count, gene-list, coefficient, enrichment, correlation, network-size, and AUC mismatches without silently adjusting parameters to force agreement.
